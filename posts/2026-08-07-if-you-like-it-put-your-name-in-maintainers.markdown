---
title: If you like it, put your name in MAINTAINERS
language: english
tags: thoughts
---

If the FLOSS project you like seems unmaintained, nominate yourself. The only
hard requirements are time and will to do the work. Skill is, in my opinion,
a secondary concern; there's probably a lot you don't know, but I assure you
there'd be plenty of chances to learn.

This is apropos to [LightDM being effectively unmaintained][lightdm] a few years
back (via [LWN][lwn]):

> I had hoped that someone might come forward who wanted to maintain it but
> I haven’t seen any interest (though I have also not actively tried to
> encourage this).

I've been on the other side of that: a curious user looking in on a project
whose issue tracker is gradually overrun with weeds. A few times, I asked if
I can be a co-maintainer. It always went well, in my opinion anyway.

The best case was when the maintainer was not yet burned out, so they stuck
around while I took up some of the work. I later stepped back from that project,
but I was smart enough to announce that and call for more maintainers. Another
contributor stepped up and continues the work.

A slightly worse case was when the maintainer already moved on, so their
presence was spotty and then tapered off. I had to fork the project in order to
make releases. It all worked out, but to me it highlights the importance of
stepping up *as soon as possible*.

A couple neutral cases was when I took up a project, worked on it for a while,
but lost interest. All of those are now collecting dust. As far as I can tell,
no harm was done, since none of those projects seem to be used by anyone.
Previous maintainers never contacted me to get the projects back either.

Given [Jia Tan][jia-tan] and other similar stories, maintainers might be
reluctant to give commit bits right away. If you're in for the long haul, it
shouldn't matter. Your help will let maintainers relax a bit, increasing their
chance of staying around and helping *you*. Then everyone benefits.
